# Post-Shoot Sorting Checklist (Public-Eligible vs. Private-Only)
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active operational checklist, designed for a creator (who may be a teenager) to actually use after a shoot. Addresses Gap #8 from the Track A hypothetical run: "No post-shoot sorting workflow for [creator] and [parent]."

**What this implements:** The Parent/Guardian Media & Consent Agreement Section 2 establishes the RULE (default to private delivery for footage with identifiable third-party minors). This checklist is the actual workflow a creator follows at delivery time to apply that rule consistently, photo by photo, without having to re-derive the logic each time.

---

## When to use this

After any shoot where other people's kids (not just your paying client) were in the area — basically any team event, tournament, or game. Go through this BEFORE delivering anything or posting anything publicly.

## The sorting pass

For each photo/clip, ask these questions in order. The first "yes" you hit tells you what bucket it goes in.

**Question 1 — Is this a photo of ONLY my paying client (the athlete/family who booked this coverage), with no other identifiable kid clearly in frame?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket.** This is the simplest case — your client, nobody else clearly identifiable.
- NO → go to Question 2.

**Question 2 — Is this a wide/action shot where other people ARE in frame, but no individual OTHER kid is a clear, identifiable subject (e.g., it's clearly about my client, others are blurred motion or background)?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket.** Per Section 2.2's first bullet — wide/action shots where no other child is a clear identifiable subject are fine to post.
- NO → go to Question 3.

**Question 3 — Did the event organizer give a blanket media-release confirmation for this event (per Section 3's "confirming with the organizer" process)?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket**, but only for content from THIS event — a blanket release for one event doesn't carry to the next one.
- NO → go to Question 4.

**Question 4 — Did the SPECIFIC other family (whose kid is identifiable) say yes when asked?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket**, but only for that specific photo/clip featuring that specific kid — doesn't extend to other content from the same event with different kids in it.
- NO → **Private-only bucket.** This is the safe default — deliver privately to your paying client only, never post publicly.

**When in doubt at any step — private-only.** Section 2.3's instruction stands: if you're not sure, default to private. It's never wrong to be more careful; it can be a real problem to be less careful.

## Practical workflow

1. **Import everything from the shoot first**, don't sort while shooting — you'll make worse decisions rushed and in the moment.
2. **Go through every photo/clip once**, tagging each with the bucket it lands in (most editing software lets you use color tags or a simple folder structure: "Public-Eligible" / "Private-Only").
3. **Deliver everything to your paying client** regardless of bucket — they get all their photos either way; the bucket only determines what YOU can additionally post publicly.
4. **Only pull from the Public-Eligible bucket** when posting to your own page or considering for any operator-reposting request.
5. **If something in Private-Only later needs to go public** (e.g., you get a late yes from another family), that's a new decision at that point — don't pre-stage things assuming a future yes.

## A note for parents helping a younger creator with this

If your creator is young enough that this judgment call feels like a lot to put on them, sit with them for the first several shoots and walk through the questions together out loud. The goal is for them to internalize the pattern, not to memorize a rulebook — after a few shoots, most creators get a good intuitive feel for which photos are obviously fine and which ones need the closer look.
