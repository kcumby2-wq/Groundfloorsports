# Post-Shoot Sorting Checklist (Public-Eligible vs. Private-Only)
**Version:** 1.1
**Status:** Active operational checklist, designed for a creator (who may be a teenager) to actually use after a shoot. Addresses Gap #8 from the Track A hypothetical run: "No post-shoot sorting workflow for [creator] and [parent]."

**What this implements:** The Parent/Guardian Media & Consent Agreement Section 2 establishes the RULE (default to private delivery for footage with identifiable third-party minors). This checklist is the actual workflow a creator follows at delivery time to apply that rule consistently, photo by photo, without having to re-derive the logic each time.

**What changed in v1.1:** Adds a fifth check — subject identity verification (jersey number or equivalent) — before content is delivered to ANY family, not just before public posting. Companion to `SUBJECT-IDENTITY-VERIFICATION-DELIVERY-QA.md` and AGV v1.2's matching check on the AI-generation side.

---

## When to use this

After any shoot where other people's kids (not just your paying client) were in the area — basically any team event, tournament, or game. Go through this BEFORE delivering anything or posting anything publicly.

## The sorting pass

For each photo/clip, ask these questions in order. The first "yes" you hit tells you what bucket it goes in.

**Question 1 — Is this a photo of ONLY my paying client (the athlete/family who booked this coverage), with no other identifiable kid clearly in frame?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket.** This is the simplest case — your client, nobody else clearly identifiable.
- NO → go to Question 2.

**Question 2 — Is this a wide/action shot where other people ARE in frame, but no individual OTHER kid is a clear, identifiable subject (e.g., it's clearly about my client, others are blurred motion or background)?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket.** Per Section 2.2's first bullet — wide/action shots where no other child is a clear identifiable subject are fine to post.
- NO → go to Question 3.

**Question 3 — Did the event organizer give a blanket media-release confirmation for this event (per Section 3's "confirming with the organizer" process)?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket**, but only for content from THIS event — a blanket release for one event doesn't carry to the next one.
- NO → go to Question 4.

**Question 4 — Did the SPECIFIC other family (whose kid is identifiable) say yes when asked?**
- YES → **Public-eligible bucket**, but only for that specific photo/clip featuring that specific kid — doesn't extend to other content from the same event with different kids in it.
- NO → **Private-only bucket.** This is the safe default — deliver privately to your paying client only, never post publicly.

**When in doubt at any step — private-only.** Section 2.3's instruction stands: if you're not sure, default to private. It's never wrong to be more careful; it can be a real problem to be less careful.

## Question 5 (NEW in v1.1) — Subject identity check, applies before ANY delivery, public or private

Before this photo/clip goes to ANY family — even your own paying client, not just before public posting — ask one more question:

**Is the jersey number (or other roster identifier) visible in this shot, and does it actually match the family/athlete I'm about to deliver this to?**

- At a multi-team event, or even within one team where numbers can be similar (e.g., 14 and 41), it's a real and easy mistake to deliver the wrong kid's action shot to a family — especially in a fast-paced sorting session with hundreds of photos.
- If the roster has jersey numbers captured (per the structured intake described in `SUBJECT-IDENTITY-VERIFICATION-DELIVERY-QA.md`), check the photo against that roster entry before it goes in this family's delivery folder.
- If you can't see the jersey number clearly enough to confirm (motion blur, number obscured, photo cropped too tight), use other context — face, hair, gear, position on field — but flag low-confidence matches for a second look rather than just shipping them.
- **This check happens regardless of which bucket (public-eligible or private-only) the photo landed in from Questions 1-4.** A misattributed photo is a problem whether or not it's also a third-party-minor privacy question — your actual paying client deserves photos of their own kid, full stop.

## Practical workflow

1. **Import everything from the shoot first**, don't sort while shooting — you'll make worse decisions rushed and in the moment.
2. **Go through every photo/clip once**, tagging each with the bucket it lands in (most editing software lets you use color tags or a simple folder structure: "Public-Eligible" / "Private-Only").
3. **Run the Question 5 identity check as you sort into each family's delivery folder** — not as a separate pass at the end, but as part of the same sorting motion, so it's not skipped under time pressure.
4. **Deliver everything to your paying client** regardless of public/private bucket — they get all their photos either way; the bucket only determines what YOU can additionally post publicly.
5. **Only pull from the Public-Eligible bucket** when posting to your own page or considering for any operator-reposting request.
6. **If something in Private-Only later needs to go public** (e.g., you get a late yes from another family), that's a new decision at that point — don't pre-stage things assuming a future yes.

## A note for parents helping a younger creator with this

If your creator is young enough that this judgment call feels like a lot to put on them, sit with them for the first several shoots and walk through the questions together out loud — including the jersey-number check, which is genuinely an easy thing to skip when you're moving fast through hundreds of photos. The goal is for them to internalize the pattern, not to memorize a rulebook — after a few shoots, most creators get a good intuitive feel for which photos are obviously fine and which ones need the closer look.
