# Parent/Guardian Media & Consent Agreement
**Version:** 1.1
**For:** Creator-Owned Coverage Businesses (CMC Visuals and future SubjectSkillz creators)
**Status:** TEMPLATE — to be reviewed with the parent/guardian and signed before stage 6 (Content), stage 7 (Automation), or stage 9 (Portal go-live) proceeds. Legal review recommended before any real signature.

**What changed in v1.1:** Added Section 5 (Operator-Side Reposting). The original v1.0 sections only addressed third-party minors visible in event footage — they did not address whether Trail of Joy / Subject Medias / Groundfloorsports may repost the creator's own work on operator-owned brand pages. The creative workflow's DSC v1.1 surfaced this gap; v1.1 of this agreement closes it.

---

## Why this document exists

Carson is shooting and selling photo/video coverage of athletes at team events, tournaments, and games. The athlete whose family books and pays for coverage has already agreed to be filmed and have their image sold — that's the transaction. But events involve other people's kids too: teammates in the background, opposing players, siblings, other families at the same field. Those families haven't separately agreed to anything. This document covers how Trail of Joy and Carson's parent/guardian agree, in writing, on how that gets handled before any of it becomes a content or automation system that runs without a person checking it.

Beyond that, v1.1 also addresses a second question: when Carson's own creative work (the photos and videos he shoots) gets reposted on Trail of Joy's own operator pages — Subject Medias, Groundfloorsports — that's a separate question from third-party-minor consent and needs its own explicit answer.

This is between **Trail of Joy** and **Carson's parent/guardian**. It is not a contract with the other families at events — it's the internal rule Carson follows so that those families' rights are respected, plus the explicit rule for how Carson's own work travels off his own pages.

---

## Section 1 — Who this covers

- **Carson McCauley**, the creator, a minor. Carson's own appearance, name, and likeness in CMC Visuals' marketing and business materials are covered by the standard creator agreement (the parent/guardian-controlled Stripe Connect account already reflects parental control of payouts).
- **Buying families** — the athlete and family who book and pay for coverage. Their consent to be filmed and to have that footage delivered and used for delivery purposes is captured at checkout (existing consent checkbox).
- **Third-party minors** — anyone else visible in event footage who is *not* the paying athlete: teammates, opposing players, siblings, bystanders.
- **(v1.1) The creator's own creative output** — the photos and videos Carson takes and the captions/copy he produces, considered as work that lives on his own brand pages by default and may travel to operator-owned pages only under the terms of Section 5.

## Section 2 — The rule for third-party minors

(unchanged from v1.0)

1. **Default to private delivery.** Footage that includes recognizable third-party minors is delivered privately to the buying family (download link, private gallery) and is **not** posted publicly (Instagram, TikTok, the CMC Visuals website, portfolio) unless Section 3 is satisfied.
2. **Public posting requires one of:**
   - The shot is a wide/action shot where no other individual child is a clear, identifiable subject, or
   - Event/team organizers have a blanket media-release policy covering all participants (common at some tournaments), or
   - The specific third-party family has separately said yes when asked.
3. **When in doubt, don't post it publicly.** Default to the buying family's private delivery.
4. **Team/group photos** for a paying team (the $399 Team Elite package) are different — the team that booked coverage has agreed as a group, so Section 2 wouldn't apply within that team's own purchased content.

## Section 3 — What "confirming with the event/organizer" actually looks like

(unchanged from v1.0)

This doesn't need to be formal every time. In practice:
- Before shooting a new event, Carson or a parent has a 2-minute conversation with the coach/organizer: "Is it okay if I post highlight content from today publicly, or should I keep it to the families who book individual coverage?"
- That answer gets noted (a text, an email, a line in the event record) so there's a record of what was agreed for that event.
- If no one asks, the safe default in Section 2.1 applies: private delivery only.

## Section 4 — What Trail of Joy will and won't automate

(unchanged from v1.0)

- The **checkout consent checkbox** (buying family agrees to be filmed, footage delivered, and used for their own order) stays as-is.
- **Public posting of any footage is not automated.** No automation system will auto-post event content to CMC Visuals' public channels. A human (Carson or his parent) reviews and decides what goes public.
- The content system (stage 6) can produce templates and captions, but the decision of *which specific photos/clips* go public is never delegated to an automated trigger.

## Section 5 — Operator-Side Reposting (NEW in v1.1)

This section addresses a question separate from Sections 2-4: when Carson's own creative work (the photos he shoots, the videos he makes, the captions he writes) gets used on Trail of Joy's own operator-owned brand pages — Subject Medias, Groundfloorsports, or any other operator-owned property — what's allowed and what's not.

**Section 5.1 — What this grants**

The parent/guardian grants Trail of Joy a **non-exclusive, revocable, attribution-required, per-piece license** to repost Carson's own creative work on operator-owned brand pages, subject to all the constraints in Sections 5.2-5.5 below.

What this is NOT: a blanket right to use Carson's work however Trail of Joy wants. What it IS: permission to repost specific pieces, in identifiable ways, with the parent able to pull anything back at any time.

**Section 5.2 — Per-piece, not blanket**

Each piece of Carson's work that gets reposted on an operator page is treated as its own permission event. Trail of Joy does NOT have a standing right to grab anything from Carson's storefront and post it to Subject Medias. The mechanism is:

- The parent indicates, per piece (via Postiz approval, email confirmation, or another agreed channel), "yes, you may repost this specific piece on this specific operator page."
- That approval covers only that piece on that page. Not a future piece. Not a different page.
- Bulk approvals are permitted (e.g. "yes, the four pieces you proposed for Subject Medias this week"), but the parent is approving the specific pieces, not a standing arrangement.

**Section 5.3 — Attribution required**

Any operator-page repost of Carson's work credits Carson by name and links back to CMC Visuals' own page or portfolio. Attribution is not optional and is not waivable for convenience. If a platform format makes attribution impractical (e.g. a story format that doesn't support links), Trail of Joy either finds a way (text overlay, in-caption credit, sticker link) or doesn't repost that piece.

**Section 5.4 — Minor-safety constraints carry forward**

Every minor-safety constraint applying to Carson's own work on his own pages also applies when that work is reposted on operator pages, plus the rules from Sections 2-4 above for any third-party minors visible in the work:

- No AI training (Soul ID or equivalent) on Carson's likeness for the operator-side repost
- No fabricated scenarios derived from Carson's footage on operator pages
- No forward-looking claims about Carson's future career outcomes in operator-page captions
- All Section 2-4 constraints regarding third-party minors visible in the footage

**Section 5.5 — Revocability, including after posting**

The parent/guardian may request the removal of any specific operator-page repost at any time, for any reason, with no requirement to show cause. Trail of Joy will remove the post from the operator page within 7 days of written notice. This right does NOT extend to platform-side artifacts outside Trail of Joy's control (e.g. screenshots others have taken, content others have downloaded), but it does extend to any operator-controlled property.

**Section 5.6 — What Trail of Joy gains from this**

To be honest about the trade-off: Trail of Joy gains the right to feature Carson's work in operator marketing, which has real value for Subject Medias' and Groundfloorsports' brand growth. In exchange, the parent retains real ongoing control rather than a one-time signature locking everything in. That asymmetry — convenience traded for parental control — is intentional, same as every other minor-safety choice in this system.
