## Source 1 — Follow-up call transcript (after the first call's gaps were flagged back to Marcus)

> Came back to Marcus after the first call. Walked him through Track C — said clearly: we can do creative work involving his athletes, but the family-side paperwork has to be his, not ours, and there are limits on what AI can do with a real kid's face.
>
> He said he actually already has model releases. He uses a release that PrimeTime Athletics drafted for him when he was working under them in 2023; he kept using it when he went independent. Sent it over — it's a one-page parent-signed form, covers photography, videography, and "any derivative media including digital editing and AI-assisted content." That last clause was added in 2024 when he had a parent ask about it directly.
>
> Asked Marcus about the Soul ID question specifically — does he want reusable AI avatars of his athletes for ongoing content? He said no, that wasn't what he was after. What he actually wants is to take real footage of his guys at training and use AI to make it look more polished — color grading, b-roll cuts, a few generated background or atmospheric shots that aren't the athletes themselves. "I want what I shot to look better, not to invent stuff that didn't happen."
>
> Confirmed Marcus IS willing to warrant in writing that he has the releases. Said he's done that before for tournaments and is "fine signing whatever."
>
> Asked again about budget. He said $2-3k for the initial setup and ~$800/month for ongoing content. He's done the math.
>
> Athletes for the first round of content: Tyrese (junior, has signed release on file), Marcus's son Jalen (8th grade, has signed release on file), and three others on the roster (releases on file). All five are mid-to-high-school football.

## Source 2 — The actual model release Marcus uses (provided as PDF, summarized)

> Two-page parent/guardian release. Includes:
> - Parent name, athlete name, date of birth fields
> - Express permission for photography, video, and "derivative media including AI-assisted or AI-generated content using my child's likeness"
> - Scope limited to "promotional and marketing materials produced by or on behalf of Capo Athletics"
> - Right to revoke at any time in writing, with 30-day removal of already-distributed materials where reasonably possible
> - Parent signature, date, contact info
>
> Notably: the release does NOT cover reusable AI character/identity training. It allows use of the athlete's likeness in individual generated assets but not training a persistent AI model on the athlete's face. This was apparently spelled out in the 2024 revision, and lines up with Track C point 4.

## Source 4 — Updated scope and constraints

- Asset types confirmed: Instagram posts (3-4/week), Reels (2/week), one monthly highlight video, sales-deck slides for parent meetings
- Cadence: weekly delivery
- Distribution: Instagram primary, occasional use in parent-meeting sales decks
- Constraints unchanged from first call: no flashy aesthetic, no non-clients in content, the word "elite" remains off-limits
- New constraint: AI-generated environments / b-roll OK; AI-generated humans in the scene with the athletes is NOT OK ("don't make a fake teammate stand next to my kid")
- Marcus does NOT want any of his athletes mic'd up audio used in AI-generated content where the AI is altering or extending what they actually said
