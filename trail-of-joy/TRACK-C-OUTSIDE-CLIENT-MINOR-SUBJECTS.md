# Track C — Outside-Client Creative Work Featuring Minor Subjects
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** ACTIVE POLICY (operational rule, not a contract). Any contract templates referenced here that get drafted later are subject to legal review before use.

**Why this exists:** The Two-Track Athlete Model (TWO-TRACK-ATHLETE-MODEL.md) covers (A) SubjectSkillz creators and (B) Groundfloorsports-filmed athletes. Both populations are minors whose families have a direct relationship with Trail of Joy. The Capo practice case exposed a third, structurally different population: **minors who are subjects in creative work for an outside business client, where the outside client — not Trail of Joy — is the one with the actual relationship to those minors and their families.** This document defines the rule for that case.

---

## The shape of this scenario

The client is an adult business — a training program, a small youth-sports brand, a coach, an event organizer. They are paying Subject Medias / Groundfloorsports / Trail of Joy for creative services (AI-generated content, locked-style assets, social content, marketing materials). The subjects in that creative work are minors who are the *client's* customers (athletes, students, members), not Trail of Joy's creators or filmed athletes.

In other words: Trail of Joy is two steps removed from the minor. The client knows the families. We don't.

That distance changes what responsible policy looks like. We can't run the same parent-facing consent process we run for our own Track A creators, because the parents aren't our parents to talk to. But we also can't pretend the distance erases the question. Generated AI imagery featuring real minor athletes — whether trained on their faces via Soul ID or rendered from reference photos — is genuinely more permanent and more publishable than a delivered photo gallery, and that doesn't change just because the contract was signed with the coach rather than the family.

## The policy

### Track C is permitted only when ALL of the following are true:

1. **The outside client has written, parent-signed model releases for every minor subject whose likeness will appear in the work**, scoped to cover AI-generated/derivative use specifically (not just "you can use my kid's photo on social media"). A generic "media release" parents sign at the start of a training program is **not sufficient** unless it explicitly addresses AI-generated or derivative imagery. If the language is ambiguous, default to "not sufficient."

2. **The outside client provides those releases to Trail of Joy on request**, and Trail of Joy retains the right to ask for them at any point during or after the engagement.

3. **The outside client warrants, in writing in their Service Agreement, that they have those releases in place and that they are responsible to the families for the use of their kids' likenesses.** Trail of Joy is the creative service provider; the client is the party with the actual relationship to the families and bears the actual obligation to them.

4. **No Soul ID training or persistent AI character profile is created from a minor subject's likeness.** This one is non-negotiable. The Soul ID model is a "locked-in identity that remains faithful across hundreds or even thousands of outputs" (Higgsfield's own description). Creating a permanent, reusable digital double of a real minor — even with parent and client consent — is a level of permanence I'm not willing to build a Track C workflow around. Track C work can use minor athletes' likenesses in *individual* generated assets (rendered from real footage they're already in), but cannot train a reusable AI identity from a minor's face. If a client like Capo specifically wants reusable AI avatars of their minor athletes, that is out of scope for Track C, full stop.

5. **The minor subject is identifiable as a real specific kid only to the extent necessary for the asset's purpose.** Background athletes in a wide gym shot don't need to be the subject. Hero imagery centered on one identifiable kid requires that specific kid's release on file, not just a roster-wide blanket release.

### Track C is NOT permitted when:

- The client does not have parent-signed releases, or refuses to confirm they have them
- The releases the client has do not cover AI/derivative imagery specifically
- The client wants Soul ID or any reusable AI identity trained on a minor's face (per point 4 above)
- The client wants generated imagery that depicts a minor in a context that didn't happen in the source footage (e.g. putting a real kid's face on an AI-generated body in a scenario the kid was never in) — this is a fabrication risk regardless of consent
- Anything about the client's program, claims, or compensation of minors suggests the kid isn't being treated well by the client themselves

### What Trail of Joy does on its end

- The client's Service Agreement includes a Track C addendum with the warranties in points 1-3 above.
- CBI screens for Track C at intake and routes accordingly: if subjects include minors and they're not Trail of Joy creators, the brief enters Track C review before any creative work proceeds.
- If at any point a Track C client cannot or will not produce the releases, the engagement pauses pending resolution. The flat-fee licensing structure from the Groundfloorsports Footage Licensing Agreement is NOT the model here — Track C is a service relationship, not a footage-ownership relationship.

## How this interacts with the existing tracks

| Population | Track | Who has the family relationship | Trail of Joy's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| SubjectSkillz creators (Carson) | A | Trail of Joy directly | Direct paperwork with the family |
| Groundfloorsports-filmed athletes | B | Trail of Joy / Groundfloorsports directly | Direct paperwork; GFS owns footage; flat-fee licensing |
| Minor subjects in outside-client creative work (Capo's athletes) | **C** | The outside client | Indirect — relies on client's warranty + parent-signed releases held by client |

These three tracks should never be combined into one document signed by anyone. A minor could conceivably appear under multiple tracks (a SubjectSkillz creator who is also a paying client of a Capo-like business that also hires Subject Medias for creative work) — in that case, each track's paperwork applies independently, none of them subsumes another.

## What this document does NOT do

- Does not create a contract. The actual Track C client Service Agreement / addendum is a separate document that needs to be drafted with legal review before use. This policy is the operational rule the agreement will implement.
- Does not make Trail of Joy responsible for verifying the genuineness of parent-signed releases beyond reasonable diligence (asking for them, reading them, noticing if something looks off). The client bears primary responsibility.
- Does not authorize Soul ID training on minor subjects. Ever. Even with consent. That's the line, and I'd rather state it plainly than leave it to "we'll see."
