# Adult Contractor ↔ Minor Footage Overlap Rule
**Status: Active policy, not a template needing legal review** — this is an internal operating rule, not a contract families sign.

**Why this exists:** Subject Media's adult videographer contractors and Trail of Joy's minor creators/athletes (Track A and Track B) can both be present at the same event. A contracted videographer might film a SubjectSkillz creator, or film an athlete who is also being covered by a Track B Groundfloorsports relationship, without anyone planning for it. This document is the one-page answer to "whose rules apply when that happens."

---

## The short version

**Contractor ownership of footage and minor-safety consent are two separate questions, and a yes to one is never a yes to the other.** The Videographer Services Agreement's work-for-hire clause (Section 5: Subject Media owns everything the contractor shoots) answers *who owns the footage*. It does not answer *whether footage of a specific minor can be posted publicly, sold, or used commercially* — that's governed by whichever Track A/B agreement (or lack of one) applies to that specific kid.

## What this means in practice

| Scenario | Who owns the footage | What governs whether it can be posted/used |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor films a SubjectSkillz creator (Track A) at an event | Subject Media, per the Videographer Agreement | The creator's own Parent/Guardian Media & Consent Agreement — same third-party-minor rules apply as if Carson had shot it himself |
| Contractor films an athlete with a Track B (Groundfloorsports footage-licensing) agreement on file | Subject Media (as the contractor's employer) for this specific footage, separately from any GFS-owned footage from other shoots | The athlete's Track B agreement terms (flat fee on commercial use) — if this footage gets used commercially, the same flat-fee obligation applies, regardless of which adult held the camera |
| Contractor films a minor with NO Track A/B agreement on file (a regular paying client at a Subject Media event, not part of either program) | Subject Media, per the standard Videographer Agreement and Subject Media's own client consent process | Subject Media's standard client terms — not a Trail of Joy minor-safety gate matter, since this is the normal commercial coverage business, not a Track A/B relationship |
| Contractor films OTHER kids in the background while covering a Track A/B athlete (teammates, opposing players, bystanders) | Subject Media | **The third-party-minor rule still applies** — default to private delivery to the purchasing family, don't post publicly unless cleared, exactly as it would if a SubjectSkillz creator had shot it |

## The one rule that never changes regardless of who's holding the camera

If footage includes a recognizable minor who is not the purchasing client, and that minor doesn't have separate consent on file, the default is **don't post it publicly.** This applies identically whether the camera was held by:
- A SubjectSkillz creator (governed by their consent agreement)
- A Subject Media adult contractor (governed by this document)
- Groundfloorsports' own crew (governed by the footage licensing agreement)

Ownership of the footage is a business/legal question. Whether a specific kid's face can go on a public page is a safety question. They are decided separately, every time.

## What this does NOT require

- This does not require contractors to ask every family at every event whether their kid is "Track A or Track B" — that's an internal Trail of Joy/Subject Media distinction, not something a contractor needs to manage on-site. Contractors just follow the existing Content Quality SOP and standard client consent process. The Track A/B distinction matters on the back end, when content is being reviewed for public posting or commercial licensing.
- This does not change the Videographer Agreement itself or anyone's pay structure. It's a clarifying rule layered on top, not a renegotiation.
