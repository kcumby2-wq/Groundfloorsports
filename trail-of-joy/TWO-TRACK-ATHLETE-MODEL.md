# Trail of Joy — Two-Track Athlete Relationship Model
**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** Makes explicit that Groundfloorsports-filmed athletes and SubjectSkillz creators are two genuinely separate relationships, with separate intake, separate paperwork, and separate ongoing involvement. A kid can be in one track, the other, both, or neither. Being filmed by Groundfloorsports does NOT automatically make someone a SubjectSkillz creator, and becoming a SubjectSkillz creator does NOT automatically put their footage under Groundfloorsports' ownership model.

**Why this needs to be its own document:** without a clear split, it's easy for the two paperwork tracks (CMC-style "you own everything" vs. Groundfloorsports "we own the footage, you get a flat fee on commercial use") to blur together for a family, for a future team member building intake, or for me in a future session without this context. This document is the thing that prevents that blur.

## The two tracks, side by side

| | **Track A: SubjectSkillz Creator** | **Track B: Groundfloorsports-Filmed Athlete** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who this is** | A young person (like Carson) who creates media — shoots, edits, sells coverage to other families through their own storefront | An athlete who Groundfloorsports films at events, games, sessions |
| **What they're agreeing to** | The Parent/Guardian Media & Consent Agreement (consent-agreement-TEMPLATE.md) — covers their own data/likeness as a creator, third-party minors in their footage, and the 80/20 storefront split | The Footage Ownership & Commercial Licensing Agreement (GROUNDFLOORSPORTS-FOOTAGE-LICENSING-AGREEMENT-TEMPLATE.md) — covers GFS owning the footage, flat one-time fee if used commercially |
| **Who owns the footage** | The creator/their family — "you own everything, no one else holds rights" (the live Subject Medias promise) | Groundfloorsports owns the camera footage; family does not get raw ownership, but gets a guaranteed flat fee if it's commercially licensed |
| **Revenue model** | 80/20 split on direct sales through their own storefront | No ongoing revenue; one-time flat fee, only if and when footage is actually licensed commercially |
| **Pipeline used** | Full MASTER-BUILD-PIPELINE.md, stages 1-10 (Bible, classification, storefront, etc.) | A lighter intake — see below. Does not need a WorldBuild Bible, a storefront, or SubjectOS classification, because there's no business being built around them |
| **Intake form** | The Creator Intake tool (creator-intake-form.html) | A separate, shorter Athlete Filming Intake (to be built — see Part 2 below) |
| **SubjectSkillz program access** | Yes — cohorts, 1-on-1s, the actual training/mentorship | No, not automatically. A Groundfloorsports-filmed athlete is not enrolled in anything; they're just an athlete who got filmed |

## Can someone be in both tracks?

Yes, and it should be treated as two separate yeses, not one. A kid could be a SubjectSkillz creator (Track A) AND get filmed by Groundfloorsports at an event in a context where GFS owns that specific footage (Track B) — those are two different relationships with two different pieces of paper, even if it's the same kid. Signing one does not imply or carry over to the other. If a family is in both, say so plainly to them: "this agreement covers the footage from Saturday's event specifically; it's separate from your creator agreement."

## Part 1 — What does NOT change between tracks

- **The minor-safety gate still applies to both.** Whether it's a SubjectSkillz creator's footage or Groundfloorsports' own filming, if other kids are recognizable in frame, the same default-to-private rule applies before anything goes public.
- **Both require a real parent/guardian signature before anything goes live** — no discretionary, no verbal-only agreements, for either track.
- **Neither track involves tokenization, NFT minting, or resellable third-party ownership stakes in an athlete's footage or moments.** This is a permanent exclusion across both tracks, not specific to one.

## Part 2 — What Track B's intake actually needs (lighter than Track A's)

Track B doesn't need the Creator Intake tool's brand/offer/social-handle fields — there's no storefront or personal brand being built. A Track B intake just needs:

1. Athlete full name + age
2. Parent/guardian name + contact
3. Sport + team/event context
4. General location
5. **The same third-party-footage flag** ("will other kids be in frame at the events you're filmed at?") — still required, same reasoning as Track A
6. Confirmation the Footage Ownership & Commercial Licensing Agreement (Section 5) has been reviewed and signed before filming starts
7. State of residence (relevant for the agreement's NIL/minor-contract variation, per that document's internal notes)

This is intentionally a much shorter form than Track A's. Building a separate lightweight version of the intake tool for this is a reasonable next step, but isn't urgent until Groundfloorsports filming is actually happening at scale.

## Part 3 — A practical rule for whoever's running intake (you, or eventually someone else)

When meeting an athlete or family for the first time, the first real question to settle internally is: **is this a SubjectSkillz conversation, a Groundfloorsports conversation, or both?** That determines which form gets filled out and which agreement gets discussed. Don't default to "sign everything just in case" — that's exactly the kind of paperwork blur this document exists to prevent. If it's just event filming with no creator program involved, Track B alone is the honest answer, and that's fine — not every relationship needs to become a SubjectSkillz enrollment.

## Exit gate for this model being "in place"
- [ ] A standalone Track B intake exists (even a simple version) distinct from the Creator Intake tool
- [ ] At least one real Track B signature has happened using the Footage Licensing Agreement, separate from any Track A paperwork
- [ ] No family has received documents that blend "you own everything" language with "we own the footage" language in the same packet
