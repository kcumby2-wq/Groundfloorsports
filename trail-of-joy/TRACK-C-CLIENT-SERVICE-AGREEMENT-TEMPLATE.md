## 1. Parties

This Agreement is entered into by and between:

**Trail of Joy / Subject Medias** ("Service Provider"), providing AI-assisted creative services including brand style locking, copy generation, asset generation, and content distribution,

and

**[Client legal name]** ("Client"), an adult business engaging Service Provider's creative services.

## 2. Scope of Services

Service Provider will, per the engagement scope agreed in the intake process:

- Produce a Client Brief (via the CBI agent) capturing Client's brand identity, voice, visual style, and constraints
- Produce a Style Lock Document (via the SLS agent) establishing Client's locked aesthetic, voice, and (where applicable) visual identity anchors
- Generate copy and caption drafts (via the CCG agent) for Client review and approval
- Generate visual/video assets (via the AGV agent) for Client review and approval
- Distribute approved content to Client-designated destinations (via the DSC agent), per Client's specified approval process

All AI-assisted outputs are drafts requiring Client (or Client's designated reviewer) approval before any public use. Service Provider does not auto-publish without an approval step appropriate to the destination, per Section 6.

## 3. Client Representations and Warranties

**3.1 — General representations.** Client represents that:
- Client has the legal right to engage Service Provider for the creative work described in this Agreement
- Client owns or has appropriate rights to any existing brand assets, reference material, or footage provided to Service Provider
- Client will provide accurate information during the intake process

**3.2 — Subject consent (applies to ALL client engagements, not only those involving minors).** Client represents that for any individual whose likeness, voice, or identifiable characteristics will appear in generated creative work:
- Client has obtained appropriate consent/release for that individual's likeness to be used in the marketing/promotional context described in this engagement
- If that consent is limited in scope (e.g., covers only certain platforms, certain uses, or excludes certain types of generated content), Client will disclose those limits to Service Provider at intake and as they change
- Client will notify Service Provider promptly if any consent is revoked or modified during the engagement

**3.3 — Accuracy of claims.** Client will not request, and Service Provider will not knowingly produce, content containing factual claims about real individuals (achievements, statistics, affiliations) that Client has not confirmed are accurate.

## 4. Service Provider's Obligations and Limits

**4.1 — Review and approval gates.** Service Provider will not publish any content to a public-facing destination without an approval step appropriate to that destination's population (see Section 6 and the Distribution & Scheduling agent's documented permission model).

**4.2 — Constraint enforcement.** Service Provider's creative agents enforce certain hard constraints that apply to every engagement and cannot be waived by Client request, including but not limited to:
- No fabrication of events, statements, or achievements not confirmed by Client
- No generation of content that would constitute harassment, discrimination, or content otherwise prohibited under Service Provider's general content policies

**4.3 — Service Provider does not guarantee specific business outcomes** (engagement, sales, follower growth) from creative work produced under this Agreement.

## 5. IP and Ownership

**5.1 — Client's brand assets remain Client's.** Logos, existing brand materials, and Client-provided reference content remain Client's property.

**5.2 — Generated creative work.** Unless otherwise specified in an addendum, Client owns the final approved creative assets produced for Client under this engagement, for use within the scope described in Client's Brief. Service Provider retains the right to reference the engagement (not the specific creative assets) in its own portfolio/case studies unless Client objects in writing.

**5.3 — Underlying agent infrastructure, prompts, and Style Lock methodology** (the CBI/SLS/CCG/AGV/DSC system itself) remain Service Provider's proprietary work product, not transferred to Client.

## 7. Reposting Rights (Cross-Population Use)

**7.1 — Default: no reposting beyond Client's own designated destinations.** Unless Section 7.2 is affirmatively elected below, Service Provider will NOT repost or feature creative work produced for Client on Service Provider's own brand pages (Subject Medias, Groundfloorsports) or in Service Provider's general marketing.

**7.2 — Optional: Service Provider reposting rights (Client elects below).**

☐ **Client grants** Service Provider a non-exclusive, revocable, attribution-required right to feature select pieces of Client's creative work on Service Provider's own brand pages, subject to:
- Per-piece Client approval before any specific repost (same per-piece model as the Track A creator agreement)
- Attribution to Client by name/handle on any repost
- Client's right to revoke any specific repost at any time, with removal within 7 business days of written notice
- **If the reposted content features a minor subject, Client additionally warrants that the underlying parent-signed release Client holds for that minor explicitly covers use beyond Client's own marketing** (see Section 8.4) — Service Provider will request confirmation of this before any such repost and will not proceed without it

☐ **Client does NOT grant** reposting rights. Content stays within Client's own designated destinations only.

## 8. Minor Subject Addendum (applies only if Client's creative work features minors who are not Trail of Joy creators/athletes — Track C)

**This section implements `TRACK-C-OUTSIDE-CLIENT-MINOR-SUBJECTS.md` v1.0 as binding contract terms.**

**8.1 — Client's release obligation.** Client represents and warrants that Client holds written, parent/guardian-signed releases for every minor subject whose likeness will appear in creative work produced under this engagement, and that those releases explicitly cover AI-generated or AI-derivative imagery (not merely general photography/video use). A generic release that does not address AI/derivative use is insufficient, and Client confirms it will not rely on one. **(See `RECOMMENDED-AI-DERIVATIVE-RELEASE-LANGUAGE.md` — Service Provider will share this with Client at intake to help close this gap.)**

**8.2 — Production on request.** Client will provide copies of the relevant releases to Service Provider upon request, at any point during or after this engagement.

**8.3 — Primary responsibility to families.** Client, not Service Provider, holds the direct relationship with the minors' families and bears primary responsibility for the appropriateness and accuracy of releases. Service Provider's role is limited to reasonable diligence (requesting releases, reviewing them for the AI-derivative-use language specifically, and pausing work if something appears off) — Service Provider does not independently verify every release's authenticity beyond that diligence.

**8.4 — Scope limitation on reposting.** Per Section 7.2, if Client elects to grant Service Provider reposting rights AND a minor subject appears in a piece Service Provider wishes to repost, Client additionally confirms the specific release for that minor covers use beyond Client's own marketing context. If it does not, that specific piece is not eligible for Section 7.2 reposting regardless of Client's general election, until/unless the release is amended or separate parent confirmation for that specific use is obtained.

**8.5 — Absolute prohibitions (non-negotiable, no Client election changes these).**
- Service Provider will NOT train any persistent AI identity (Soul ID or equivalent) on any minor subject's likeness, regardless of Client consent or request.
- Service Provider will NOT generate content depicting a minor subject in a fabricated scenario not derivable from real source footage Client has provided.
- Service Provider will NOT generate content making forward-looking claims about a minor subject's future career, recruiting, or professional outcomes.
- Service Provider will NOT generate AI-altered audio/speech attributed to a minor subject.

**8.6 — Engagement pause on release failure.** If at any point Service Provider has reason to believe Client's releases are insufficient, revoked, or were never actually obtained, Service Provider will pause creative work involving the affected minor subject(s) pending resolution. This is not a breach remedy reserved for after the fact — it is a standing operational right Service Provider exercises proactively.

## Internal notes (not part of the signed page)

1. **This is the contract `TRACK-C-OUTSIDE-CLIENT-MINOR-SUBJECTS.md` v1.0 referenced but didn't build.** Section 8 of this Agreement implements that policy as binding terms. Do not deploy Section 8 without legal review — minor-subject liability terms are exactly the kind of clause that needs real legal eyes, not just careful drafting.
2. **Section 7's checkbox structure is deliberate.** It defaults to NO reposting rights, requiring an affirmative election to grant them. This mirrors the same "opt-in, not opt-out" structure used in the Track A consent agreement's Section 5 — convenience for Service Provider is never the default; it has to be specifically granted.
3. **This Agreement is what the Capo practice case was missing.** Without it, DSC v1.1's cross-population permission check for Track C content (Section 7) has no actual contract to point to, which means the check can never pass for a real Track C client — exactly the gap that's now closed.
4. **For Capo specifically**, if this were a real engagement: Section 8.1 would require Marcus's existing parent release language to be reviewed against the "explicitly covers AI-generated/derivative use" standard (per the practice case material, his release does include that language as of a 2024 revision — promising, but still needs real verification, not just a templated read).
5. **(v1.1) Reasoning behind the Section 2.1 fee tiers.** Researched current market rates for small-business social media/creative content services before proposing numbers: basic packages for small businesses with 1-2 platforms generally run $500-$1,500/month, with full-service multi-platform packages at $2,000-$5,000+/month for larger accounts. The tiers above are positioned at the lower-to-middle end of that range — appropriate for the actual client profile this Agreement targets (small training programs and youth sports businesses like Capo or Verde Athletics, not mid-market or enterprise accounts). The setup fee reflects the real one-time work of CBI/SLS intake and style-lock construction, which doesn't repeat monthly. These numbers should be treated as a reasoned starting point, not a final settled price — adjust based on actual delivery costs (including Higgsfield credit usage) and competitive positioning once you have a few real engagements under your belt.
6. **The minimum-term addition (Section 2.1 + Section 10) is new in v1.1** and reflects standard retainer-services practice — the setup/ramp-up work in Section 2 has real cost regardless of term length, and a 1-month client doesn't let that cost amortize. This is a real business protection, not boilerplate.
