# Mentor Assignment & Matching SOP
**Version:** 1.0
**Status: Active operational SOP.** Pairs with the Mentor Assignment Addendum mechanism in `SUBJECTSKILLZ-MENTORSHIP-AGREEMENT-TEMPLATE.md` v1.1. Addresses Gap #6 from the Track A hypothetical run: "no documented Mentor assignment process / pool / matching criteria — HIGH AT SCALE."

**Scope note:** This SOP governs Model B (1-on-1 mentorship) specifically, per `SUBJECTSKILLZ-EDUCATION-DELIVERY-ARCHITECTURE.md`. It does not apply to Model A (course/portal platform), which has no per-creator Mentor assignment need.

---

## Why this exists

The Mentorship Agreement establishes the contract and the safeguards. It does not establish where Mentors come from, how many you need, who decides which Mentor goes with which Mentee, or what happens when supply runs short. At a waitlist of 50 kids, this is the part of SubjectSkillz most likely to break first — not because the paperwork is wrong, but because nobody has built the operational machine the paperwork assumes exists.

## 1. The Mentor Pool

**1.1 — Pool composition.** A Mentor pool entry requires, at minimum:
- Background check completed (per a documented standard — see Section 2)
- At least 2 references checked, ideally from prior youth-facing work (coaching, teaching, youth ministry, etc.)
- A short application/interview confirming the Mentor understands and accepts the Section 3 conduct requirements from the Mentorship Agreement
- Relevant domain knowledge (sports background, content creation background, or business/entrepreneurship background, depending on what the Mentee population needs)
- Capacity stated upfront: how many Mentees can this person realistically take on (most will be 1-3 at a time, not more, given this is mentorship not mass instruction)

**1.2 — Pool sourcing.** Realistic sources, roughly in order of trust/efficiency:
- Existing Trail of Joy network (coaches, former athletes, content creators already known to the operator)
- Referrals from existing Mentors (a vetted Mentor recommending someone they know is a faster trust signal than a cold application)
- Open application (lowest trust signal, requires the most diligence — background check and references become load-bearing here, not optional)

**1.3 — Pool capacity tracking.** A simple running count: total Mentor capacity (sum of each Mentor's stated capacity) vs. current Mentee demand (creators awaiting assignment + creators with an active Mentor). **When demand exceeds capacity, new Mentee onboarding into Model B should be paused or queued — not silently overcommitted by assigning more Mentees per Mentor than they signed up for.** This is the single most important operational discipline in this entire SOP: capacity tracking has to be a real number someone checks, not a feeling.

## 2. Background & Reference Verification (referenced by Mentorship Agreement Section 3)

**2.1 — Minimum standard.** Before any Mentor is added to the pool:
- A criminal background check (the same standard used broadly across youth sports/coaching contexts — a basic state + national check at minimum; many states have a specific standard for anyone working with minors, and the Program Provider should use whichever standard applies in its operating state, with legal input on what's actually required vs. best practice)
- Two reference contacts, at least one from a context involving minors if the Mentor has that history; if not, two professional references plus a documented conversation about why they want to mentor

**2.2 — Documentation.** Each Mentor's file includes: background check date and result, reference contact names/dates/notes, application/interview notes, capacity stated, areas of expertise. This file should exist BEFORE the Mentor is assigned to any Mentee — not started after.

**2.3 — Re-verification cadence.** Background checks should be refreshed periodically (annually is a common standard) for any Mentor remaining active in the pool, not done once and assumed permanent.

## 3. Matching Criteria

When a new Mentee needs a Mentor assigned (per Mentorship Agreement Section 1.2), matching considers, in rough priority order:

**3.1 — Domain relevance.** A football-focused creator probably benefits more from a Mentor with sports/content background than a generalist. Match on what the Mentee is actually trying to build.

**3.2 — Mentor capacity availability.** Only match against Mentors who have stated open capacity per Section 1.3. Do not assign past stated capacity even under pressure to "just make it work" — this is exactly how mentorship quality erodes at scale.

**3.3 — Geographic/scheduling compatibility,** if sessions are in-person or time-zone-sensitive for live virtual sessions.

**3.4 — Parent preference, if stated.** Some parents will have a preference (gender of Mentor, age range, specific background) — this should be asked at intake, not assumed.

**3.5 — Avoid assigning a Mentor with any known prior connection to the family that could create a conflict of interest** (e.g., a Mentor who is also the Mentee's actual coach at school, which could create dual-relationship complications) unless the family explicitly wants that continuity and the Program Provider is comfortable with the overlap.

## 4. The Assignment Workflow

1. **Mentee's base Mentorship Agreement is signed** (per v1.1, with Mentor as TBD).
2. **Program Provider checks pool capacity** (Section 1.3). If no Mentor has open capacity matching the Mentee's domain, the Mentee enters a documented queue — not silence, not an indefinite "we'll get to it." The family is told honestly: "we're matching you with a Mentor and expect this within [X] business days" — and that timeframe should be real, not aspirational.
3. **A specific Mentor is proposed** based on Section 3 criteria.
4. **The Mentor Assignment Addendum is executed** (Mentor signs, Program Provider confirms background/reference verification is complete and current).
5. **Parent/Guardian is notified** of the assignment — name, brief background, and a reminder that they can request a different Mentor at any time, no justification needed (per Mentorship Agreement Section 3).
6. **First mentorship session is scheduled,** following Section 3's conduct requirements (no unsupervised 1-on-1 without another adult present/reachable, Program-approved channels only).

## 5. Reassignment

**5.1 — Parent-initiated.** Per Mentorship Agreement Section 3, a parent can request a different Mentor at any time without justification. When this happens: thank them, don't probe for reasons unless they volunteer them, find a new match per Section 3's criteria, execute a new Addendum.

**5.2 — Mentor-initiated.** A Mentor may need to step back (capacity, personal circumstances). Document the reason internally (not necessarily shared with the family beyond "your Mentor is no longer available, here's who's next"), find a replacement.

**5.3 — Conduct-triggered.** Per Mentorship Agreement Section 3's reporting requirement, any conduct concern results in immediate review and, if warranted, removal from the pool entirely (not just reassignment away from one family) — a Mentor who's a concern for one Mentee is a concern for the pool generally.

## 6. Capacity Planning for Growth

**6.1 — The waitlist math.** If the waitlist is real and growing, the Mentor pool needs to grow ahead of demand, not in reaction to it. A rough planning rule: maintain pool capacity at roughly 130-150% of current active Mentee count, so there's room to match new Mentees without an immediate queue. (This ratio is a starting assumption, not a researched figure — adjust based on real onboarding pace once you have a few months of data.)

**6.2 — Recruitment trigger.** When pool capacity utilization crosses a threshold (e.g., 80% of total capacity in active use), that's the signal to actively recruit new Mentors — not wait until the pool is already maxed and new Mentees are stuck in queue.

**6.3 — What this SOP does NOT solve.** This document gives you the process. It doesn't solve the actual hard problem of *finding* enough qualified, vetted adults willing to mentor youth creators for whatever compensation structure you settle on (Section 4.3 of the Mentorship Agreement — Mentor compensation is a separate, real business decision). If Mentor compensation is $0 (volunteer-only), pool growth will be slow and trust-network-dependent. If there's real compensation, pool growth can be faster but costs money. That trade-off is yours to make; this SOP just gives you the operational structure once you've made it.

## Exit gate for this SOP being operational
- [ ] At least 3 Mentors in the pool with complete files (Section 2.2) before the first real Mentee assignment happens
- [ ] Capacity tracking number exists somewhere real (even a simple spreadsheet) and gets checked before each new assignment
- [ ] The queue communication to families (Section 4, step 2) is a real practice, not improvised each time
