# Mentor Assignment & Matching SOP
**Version:** 1.1
**Status: Active operational SOP.** Pairs with the Mentor Assignment Addendum mechanism in `SUBJECTSKILLZ-MENTORSHIP-AGREEMENT-TEMPLATE.md` v1.1. Addresses Gap #6 from the Track A hypothetical run.

**What changed in v1.1:** Corrects v1.0's assumption that the Mentor pool was a from-scratch recruiting problem. It is not. Subject Media already runs an active Crew Hub (on ClientClub.net) of adult contractors operating under the Videographer Services Agreement, and Trail of Joy has a $10K investment-level partnership with XpandSports.com that gives meaningful reach into adjacent youth-sports networks. The pool sourcing section is rewritten to reflect that real pipeline rather than the generic "where do mentors come from?" framing of v1.0.

**Scope note:** This SOP governs Model B (1-on-1 mentorship) specifically, per `SUBJECTSKILLZ-EDUCATION-DELIVERY-ARCHITECTURE.md`. It does not apply to Model A (course/portal platform), which has no per-creator Mentor assignment need.

## Why this exists (v1.1 — corrected framing)

The Mentorship Agreement establishes the contract and the safeguards. It does not establish how Mentor sourcing actually works in this operation, how matching gets decided, or what happens when supply runs short. v1.0 of this SOP treated Mentor sourcing as a generic recruiting problem; that framing was wrong. The real sourcing pipeline is:

- **Primary funnel:** The Subject Media Crew Hub — an active community of 22+ adult contractors already operating under the Videographer Services Agreement, already proving execution standards at real events (PREMIER EVENTS, RED ZONE ELITE, Hero Salt Lake City, etc.), already inside a leaderboard system and shared SOPs. These are pre-vetted adults who understand the operating standard and have an active commercial relationship with the operator.
- **Secondary funnel:** The XpandSports relationship. A $10K investment-level partnership gives Trail of Joy real reach into XpandSports' broader youth-sports network — coaches, athletes' families, organizational contacts — adjacent to but outside the immediate Crew Hub.
- **Tertiary funnel:** Open application from outside both of the above.

This isn't a "where do we find Mentors?" problem. It is a "how do we route already-known adults into a Mentor role appropriately, with the right additional safeguards layered on top of what they already passed to be in the Crew Hub?" problem. Different problem; different solution.

## 1. The Mentor Pool

**1.1 — Pool composition.** A Mentor pool entry requires, at minimum:
- Background check completed (per a documented standard — see Section 2)
- At least 2 references checked, ideally from prior youth-facing work (coaching, teaching, youth ministry, etc.)
- A short application/interview confirming the Mentor understands and accepts the Section 3 conduct requirements from the Mentorship Agreement
- Relevant domain knowledge (sports background, content creation background, or business/entrepreneurship background, depending on what the Mentee population needs)
- Capacity stated upfront: how many Mentees can this person realistically take on (most will be 1-3 at a time, not more, given this is mentorship not mass instruction)

**1.2 — Pool sourcing (rewritten in v1.1 to reflect the real pipeline).**

**Primary: the Subject Media Crew Hub.** This is the main funnel. Adult contractors already in the Crew Hub have already cleared a real bar — they signed the Videographer Services Agreement, they're operating under documented standards (SOP 1: Sales/Clients/Delivery, SOP 2: How It Works, SOP 3: Content Quality), they show up on a leaderboard tracking performance, and they have a commercial track record covering real events. **None of that is a substitute for the additional Mentor-specific safeguards in Section 2** (a Crew Hub vetting is for an adult-to-adult commercial relationship; mentoring a minor requires a different and stricter check), but it's a meaningful trust signal at the front end.

In practice, this means a Crew Hub contractor who's been delivering reliably for 6+ months, with strong leaderboard activity, can be invited to apply to the Mentor pool. The conversation is short ("would you be interested in mentoring a SubjectSkillz creator?") because the operating-standard piece is already established. The additional vetting steps (background check refresh, reference check specifically about youth-facing work, conduct-requirements interview) are layered on top.

**Secondary: the XpandSports network.** Through the active partnership, Trail of Joy can ask XpandSports leadership for referrals from their adjacent youth-sports network — coaches who run programs at events Subject Media covers, families of athletes XpandSports has long-standing relationships with, organizational contacts the partnership opens doors to. These are warm leads with a real referral signal, but they require the full vetting standard since they're entering from outside the Crew Hub's existing operating relationship.

**Tertiary: outside referrals from existing Mentors.** Once the pool has its first cohort, vetted Mentors recommending people they know becomes a real source. This is slower-growing than the two above but produces strong matches when it happens, because the referring Mentor has already internalized the standards and is filtering at the front end.

**Quaternary: open application from outside all of the above.** This is the lowest-trust signal and requires the most diligence — background check and references become entirely load-bearing, no operating-standard signal is already in place. Should be used sparingly; the Crew Hub and XpandSports funnels should cover most needs.

**1.3 — Pool capacity tracking.** A simple running count: total Mentor capacity (sum of each Mentor's stated capacity) vs. current Mentee demand (creators awaiting assignment + creators with an active Mentor). **When demand exceeds capacity, new Mentee onboarding into Model B should be paused or queued — not silently overcommitted by assigning more Mentees per Mentor than they signed up for.** This is the single most important operational discipline in this entire SOP: capacity tracking has to be a real number someone checks, not a feeling.

## 2. Background & Reference Verification (referenced by Mentorship Agreement Section 3)

**2.1 — Minimum standard.** Before any Mentor is added to the pool, regardless of which funnel they came in through (Section 1.2):
- A criminal background check (the same standard used broadly across youth sports/coaching contexts — a basic state + national check at minimum; many states have a specific standard for anyone working with minors, and the Program Provider should use whichever standard applies in its operating state, with legal input on what's actually required vs. best practice)
- Two reference contacts, at least one from a context involving minors if the Mentor has that history; if not, two professional references plus a documented conversation about why they want to mentor

**This applies to Crew Hub contractors entering the Mentor pool as well.** The Videographer Services Agreement and Crew Hub vetting were sufficient for an adult-to-adult commercial relationship. They are NOT sufficient on their own for mentoring a minor. The additional vetting here is mandatory, not optional, regardless of how trusted a contractor is in the Crew Hub context.

**2.2 — Documentation.** Each Mentor's file includes: background check date and result, reference contact names/dates/notes, application/interview notes, capacity stated, areas of expertise, and (if applicable) note of which funnel they entered through. This file should exist BEFORE the Mentor is assigned to any Mentee — not started after.

**2.3 — Re-verification cadence.** Background checks should be refreshed periodically (annually is a common standard) for any Mentor remaining active in the pool, not done once and assumed permanent.

## 3. Matching Criteria

When a new Mentee needs a Mentor assigned (per Mentorship Agreement Section 1.2), matching considers, in rough priority order:

**3.1 — Domain relevance.** A football-focused creator probably benefits more from a Mentor with sports/content background than a generalist. Match on what the Mentee is actually trying to build. Crew Hub contractors are particularly well-suited here because their existing work covering sports events maps directly onto what most SubjectSkillz creators are building.

**3.2 — Mentor capacity availability.** Only match against Mentors who have stated open capacity per Section 1.3. Do not assign past stated capacity even under pressure to "just make it work" — this is exactly how mentorship quality erodes at scale.

**3.3 — Geographic/scheduling compatibility,** if sessions are in-person or time-zone-sensitive for live virtual sessions.

**3.4 — Parent preference, if stated.** Some parents will have a preference (gender of Mentor, age range, specific background) — this should be asked at intake, not assumed.

**3.5 — Avoid assigning a Mentor with any known prior connection to the family that could create a conflict of interest** (e.g., a Mentor who is also the Mentee's actual coach at school, which could create dual-relationship complications) unless the family explicitly wants that continuity and the Program Provider is comfortable with the overlap. Also relevant for Crew Hub Mentors who've covered events the Mentee's team participated in — incidental coverage is fine, but if there's a closer working relationship, surface it.

## 4. The Assignment Workflow

1. **Mentee's base Mentorship Agreement is signed** (per v1.1, with Mentor as TBD).
2. **Program Provider checks pool capacity** (Section 1.3). If no Mentor has open capacity matching the Mentee's domain, the Mentee enters a documented queue — not silence, not an indefinite "we'll get to it." The family is told honestly: "we're matching you with a Mentor and expect this within [X] business days" — and that timeframe should be real, not aspirational.
3. **A specific Mentor is proposed** based on Section 3 criteria.
4. **The Mentor Assignment Addendum is executed** (Mentor signs, Program Provider confirms background/reference verification is complete and current per Section 2.1).
5. **Parent/Guardian is notified** of the assignment — name, brief background, and a reminder that they can request a different Mentor at any time, no justification needed (per Mentorship Agreement Section 3).
6. **First mentorship session is scheduled,** following Section 3's conduct requirements (no unsupervised 1-on-1 without another adult present/reachable, Program-approved channels only).

## 5. Reassignment

**5.1 — Parent-initiated.** Per Mentorship Agreement Section 3, a parent can request a different Mentor at any time without justification. When this happens: thank them, don't probe for reasons unless they volunteer them, find a new match per Section 3's criteria, execute a new Addendum.

**5.2 — Mentor-initiated.** A Mentor may need to step back (capacity, personal circumstances). Document the reason internally (not necessarily shared with the family beyond "your Mentor is no longer available, here's who's next"), find a replacement.

**5.3 — Conduct-triggered.** Per Mentorship Agreement Section 3's reporting requirement, any conduct concern results in immediate review and, if warranted, removal from the Mentor pool entirely (not just reassignment away from one family) — a Mentor who's a concern for one Mentee is a concern for the pool generally. **A Mentor removed from the pool for a conduct concern should also be reviewed for status in the Crew Hub**, since the Crew Hub is the primary funnel — a serious conduct issue in the Mentor role is information the Crew Hub operating context needs to know about, not silo'd to the Mentor pool only.

## 6. Capacity Planning for Growth

**6.1 — The waitlist math.** If the waitlist is real and growing, the Mentor pool needs to grow ahead of demand, not in reaction to it. A rough planning rule: maintain pool capacity at roughly 130-150% of current active Mentee count, so there's room to match new Mentees without an immediate queue. (This ratio is a starting assumption — adjust based on real onboarding pace once you have a few months of data.)

**6.2 — Recruitment trigger.** When pool capacity utilization crosses a threshold (e.g., 80% of total capacity in active use), that's the signal to actively work the funnels — start by inviting more Crew Hub contractors to apply, then leverage the XpandSports network for warm referrals, then existing-Mentor referrals.

**6.3 — What this SOP gives you, and what's still a real decision (v1.1 — rewritten).** v1.0 of this SOP framed the hard part as "finding qualified, vetted adults willing to mentor youth creators." That framing was wrong — the Crew Hub and XpandSports partnership are real existing infrastructure that provides the supply side. The actual remaining decisions are:

- **Mentor compensation structure for the Mentor role specifically.** Crew Hub contractors are already compensated for event coverage work under the Videographer Services Agreement. Whether mentoring a SubjectSkillz creator is a stipend on top, a separate paid role, a portion-of-the-creator-revenue arrangement, or a volunteer add-on for trusted contractors — that's a real business decision (and Section 4.3 of the Mentorship Agreement keeps it deliberately separate from the family-facing terms). The funnel exists; the compensation model that makes Crew Hub contractors actually want to take on the Mentor role is what determines whether the funnel actually flows.
- **Rate-of-conversion expectations.** Not every Crew Hub contractor will want to mentor a minor, and that's fine. Plan around a realistic conversion rate (probably 20-40% of active contractors interested in being asked, then a fraction of those actually completing the additional vetting) rather than assuming the whole Crew Hub is the Mentor pool.

## Exit gate for this SOP being operational
- [ ] At least 3 Mentors in the pool with complete files (Section 2.2) before the first real Mentee assignment happens
- [ ] Capacity tracking number exists somewhere real (even a simple spreadsheet) and gets checked before each new assignment
- [ ] The queue communication to families (Section 4, step 2) is a real practice, not improvised each time
- [ ] (v1.1) The Mentor compensation decision has been made before the first invitation goes out to a Crew Hub contractor — so the conversation is "here's what mentoring would look like, including how it works compensation-wise" rather than "would you be interested?... we'll figure that part out later"
