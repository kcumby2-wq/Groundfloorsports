# Track A — Operator-Produced Content Featuring a Creator (Per-Project Consent Path)
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active policy + template. Addresses Gap #10 from the Track A hypothetical run: "No documented consent path for 'Subject Medias creates new content using a SubjectSkillz creator as the subject.'"

**What this is distinct from:** The Track A consent agreement's Section 5 (Operator-Side Reposting) covers Trail of Joy reposting a creator's OWN work (the photos/videos the creator themselves made) on operator-owned pages. This document covers a different situation: Subject Medias or Groundfloorsports producing NEW content where the creator is the SUBJECT, not the author — e.g., a "meet the SubjectSkillz creators" video, a behind-the-scenes piece, a profile feature. The creator didn't make this content; the operator did, and the creator appears in it.

---

## Why this needs its own path

The existing Track A consent agreement assumes the creator is the one making content. Section 5 governs reposting that content. Neither section addresses what happens when the relationship flips — when an adult videographer (operating under the Videographer Services Agreement) films the creator FOR a piece Subject Medias is producing about its own program. That's a third, distinct kind of content event, and it deserves its own clear consent step rather than being squeezed into a section that wasn't written for it.

## The rule

**Any time Subject Medias or Groundfloorsports wants to produce new content using a Track A creator as the subject, that requires its own per-project parent consent — not a standing right, regardless of what's already been signed in the base consent agreement or its Section 5 amendment.**

This is consistent with the same principle running through every other minor-safety decision in this system: a parent's broad "yes" to one kind of use (their kid's own content being reposted) shouldn't quietly extend to a different kind of use (their kid being filmed as a subject in someone else's production) without a fresh, specific yes.

## What per-project consent covers

Before filming/producing begins, the parent is told, in plain language:

1. **What the content is** — e.g., "a short video featuring 3-4 SubjectSkillz creators talking about their experience, for Subject Medias' own Instagram and website."
2. **Who's filming it** — which contractor, under what agreement (the Videographer Services Agreement governs the contractor's side).
3. **Where it will be used** — specific destinations (Subject Medias' IG, the website, etc.), not an open-ended "our marketing."
4. **What the creator will be asked to do** — answer questions on camera, demonstrate their work, etc. Not asked to perform a scripted scenario that isn't authentically theirs.
5. **The same minor-safety constraints that apply everywhere else in this system** — no fabricated scenarios, no forward-looking claims about the creator's future career inserted into the piece, no AI-altered audio.
6. **Revocability** — same as Section 5.5 of the base consent agreement: the parent can request removal of the finished piece at any time, with removal within 7 business days.

## The mechanism

1. Subject Medias identifies a project that will feature one or more Track A creators as subjects.
2. For each creator's parent, a short per-project consent is sent (email, a simple form, or verbal-with-written-followup) covering the six points above.
3. The parent says yes or no. No pressure, no assumption of yes from silence — if a parent doesn't respond, that creator isn't included.
4. Filming/production proceeds only for creators whose parents said yes.
5. The signed/confirmed per-project consents are saved alongside the project (e.g., `pipeline-runs/[creator-name]/operator-produced-content-consent-[project-name]-[date].md`).

## What this does NOT require

- This does NOT require re-executing the full base consent agreement. It's a lighter-weight, project-specific confirmation — the base agreement and Section 5 amendment stay as the foundational documents; this is a per-use add-on.
- This does NOT apply to a creator's own self-produced content, even if Subject Medias later reposts it (that's Section 5's territory, already covered).
- This does NOT apply to incidental appearances (e.g., a creator visible in the background of a wide shot at a Crew Hub event that wasn't about them specifically) — use judgment; if the creator IS the subject of the piece, this path applies; if they're incidentally visible, ordinary third-party-minor handling applies instead.
