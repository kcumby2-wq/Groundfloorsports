# Recommended Parent/Guardian Release Language — AI/Derivative Use
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Active reference template. Addresses Gap #2 from the Track C hypothetical run: "No 'Recommended AI-derivative Release Language' template for clients."

**What this is:** Language that Trail of Joy / Subject Medias provides to outside Track C clients (training programs, small youth sports businesses) to help them get their own parent-signed releases up to the standard required by `TRACK-C-OUTSIDE-CLIENT-MINOR-SUBJECTS.md` Section 8.1 and the Track C Client Service Agreement Section 8.1.

**What this is NOT:** A document Trail of Joy presents to a client's families directly. The client (e.g., Capo Athletics, Verde Athletics) is the party with the actual family relationship — they collect this release, in their own name, on their own letterhead. Trail of Joy provides the language; the client owns the relationship and the document.

---

## Why this exists

The Track C policy and Service Agreement both require that a client's parent-signed releases "explicitly cover AI-generated or AI-derivative imagery (not merely general photography/video use)." In practice, almost no small business owner's existing release was written with that language. Most use a generic photo/video release from a coaching certification body, a league template, or something they found online years ago. **Without a recommended template, every Track C client has to either write this language themselves (usually badly) or keep using insufficient releases and hope nobody notices.** Both outcomes are bad. This template solves it.

## How to use this

Trail of Joy provides this language to a Track C client at intake (per the discovery-call flow), with a short cover note explaining why their existing release likely isn't sufficient and what to do about it. The client either:

- **Adds this language as an addendum/amendment** to their existing release, getting parents to sign the addendum (faster than full re-signature collection), or
- **Adopts this language wholesale** into a new release going forward for new athletes/seasons, while handling the addendum approach for existing rosters

Either path works. What matters is that by the time Trail of Joy's creative agents (CBI onward) treat a minor subject as "Track C compliant," the underlying release actually contains this language, not just a general "we can take photos" permission.

## The recommended language (for the client to adapt into their own release/addendum)

> **AI-Generated and Derivative Media Use**
>
> In addition to standard photography and video use described above, I understand that [Client Organization Name] works with creative service providers who may use artificial intelligence (AI) tools to edit, enhance, or generate promotional and marketing content using my child's likeness, including but not limited to: AI-assisted photo and video editing, color grading, background/environment generation, and AI-generated marketing assets derived from real photos and video of my child captured during [Client Organization Name]'s programs and events.
>
> I give permission for this kind of AI-assisted and AI-derivative use, subject to the following limits, which [Client Organization Name] and its creative service providers agree to honor:
>
> - No persistent AI model, digital character, or "AI twin" will be trained on my child's face or likeness for reuse across future content. Any AI assistance applies to specific, individual pieces of content, not a reusable digital double of my child.
> - No content will be created showing my child in a scenario, achievement, or event that did not actually happen. AI-assisted content must be derived from real photos or video of my child taken during an actual [Client Organization Name] activity.
> - No content will make claims or predictions about my child's future athletic career, recruiting outcome, or professional prospects.
> - No AI-altered or AI-generated audio will be created using my child's voice or attributed to my child as something they said.
>
> I understand I may withdraw this specific AI/derivative-use permission at any time by notifying [Client Organization Name] in writing, and that any already-published content using my child's likeness will be reviewed and removed where reasonably possible within a reasonable timeframe of my request.
>
> This permission is for use in [Client Organization Name]'s own promotional and marketing materials. [Separate language below if the client wants to also cover Trail of Joy/Subject Medias reposting — see "Scope Extension" section.]

## Scope Extension language (only needed if the client wants Section 7 reposting rights under the Service Agreement)

If a Track C client wants to elect the optional reposting rights in the Service Agreement (their content appearing on Trail of Joy / Subject Medias' own brand pages), the release needs ADDITIONAL language beyond the base template above — the base template only covers the client's OWN use. Recommended addition:

> **Additional Use by Creative Service Providers**
>
> I understand that [Client Organization Name] may, at its discretion, permit its creative service provider ([Trail of Joy / Subject Medias]) to feature select pieces of this AI-assisted content on the service provider's own marketing channels, with attribution back to [Client Organization Name], subject to the same limits described above and subject to [Client Organization Name] notifying me before any specific piece is used this way and seeking my separate confirmation for that specific piece.

**This is deliberately a SEPARATE, additional clause, not folded into the base language.** Per Track C Service Agreement Section 8.4, this distinction is what determines whether a specific piece of content is eligible for cross-population reposting under DSC's permission check. A client whose families only signed the base template cannot elect Section 7 reposting for minor-subject content without going back for this additional confirmation — that's intentional, not an oversight.

## What this template explicitly does NOT try to solve

- **This is not a legally-finalized release.** It's recommended plain-language content for a client's own attorney (or Trail of Joy's, if Trail of Joy wants to offer that as part of onboarding) to incorporate into a properly executed release under the client's own state's law.
- **This does not replace the underlying Track C Service Agreement's warranties.** The client still warrants, in the Service Agreement itself, that releases exist and cover this use — this template is the tool to help that warranty be true, not a substitute for it.
- **This does not address non-AI-related liability/release language** (general injury waivers, photo/video use generally) — that's whatever the client already has; this template only adds the AI-specific gap.
