How recruiting actually works

The real sequence — from junior year film to a signed letter of intent. Most athletes skip or rush every one of these phases.

Phase 1
Build your film base
Recruiting starts on film, not at camps. Before you reach out to a single coach, you need game tape that shows assignment discipline — not just highlight plays. Recruiters watch film first and highlights second.
  • Submit every competitive game, not just your best ones
  • Film should show your full role — blocking, routes, effort, footwork
  • Hudl, DVSports, and QwikCut are all accepted — quality matters more than the platform
Phase 2
Get your transcript graded
A player transcript converts raw footage into a shareable document that every D1, D2, and JUCO coach knows how to read. Without one, you're asking coaches to watch 45 minutes of film and figure it out themselves — they won't.
  • Transcripts are graded by trained analysts, not AI or fan opinion
  • Each grade has a clip linked — coaches can verify exactly what they're evaluating
  • The format mirrors how college staffs already grade prospects internally
Phase 3
Build your target list
Most athletes send 200 generic emails and hear back from nobody. The athletes who get recruited build a smart target list — 15 to 30 programs at the right level, right division, right fit — and reach out with content that's actually relevant.
  • Position group needs, not program prestige, should drive your list
  • Schools outside the top 25 often offer better playing time and the same diploma
  • Class year and eligibility windows matter more than most athletes realize
Phase 4
Start outreach the right way
The first message to a coach is not the time to ask for a scholarship. It's a brief, professional introduction: your transcript link, your class year, a direct question about their needs at your position. Three sentences. Subject line matters most.
  • Coaches delete long emails — keep the first message under 100 words
  • Reference something specific about their program, not just the school name
  • Follow up once after two weeks — then move on if there's no response
Phase 5
Convert interest to a visit
Once a coach responds, your job shifts to scheduling an unofficial or official visit. This is where preparation separates the athletes who get offered from the athletes who get ghosted after a campus tour.
  • Prepare 3–5 questions that show you've researched the program's system
  • Unofficial visits are self-funded — budget and plan ahead
  • Your family's read of the program matters — include them whenever possible

Questions we get

Answered honestly

What's the difference between this and a single graded game?
A single-game transcript ($249) grades one game and hands you the PDF. The Full Player Evaluation ($999) grades a body of film, produces the composite and the trend across it, audits everything else a coach asks for, and comes with a four-week plan and a review call. One is a document; this is a diagnosis. Above it, the Verified Season ($1,500–$4,500/season) is where the record compounds across the year, and the Development Retainer ($1,200/mo) keeps it current with two calls a month.
Does the $999 count toward anything?
It credits in full toward a Verified Season if you start within 30 days of the review call. After that it doesn't — if it credited forever, the evaluation would just be a sales call with an invoice attached.
What if we just want to do it ourselves after the report?
Good — that's a real outcome, and about four in ten families take it. The report is yours to keep, with the four-week plan, and we'll check back at thirty days either way.
How does the Development Retainer work?
The $1,200/month Development Retainer is designed for athletes after a Verified Season. Two calls a month with a high-level trainer, with the record kept current between them — ongoing composite updates as new data comes in. Cancel anytime.
Who actually grades the film?
Your film is graded by trained analysts inside the Optimum Grading platform — the same platform 350+ college and HS coaching staffs use for team grading. Every play gets scored across assignment, technique, effort, and execution, and every grade is linked to the exact clip.
Why do I need to book a call first?
Recruiting is personal. Before we take your money, we want to understand your position, class year, goals, and current situation — so we can tell you honestly whether the program is the right fit, and which package makes sense. No pressure, no hard sell.
Is this NIL-compliant?
Yes. All client agreements are structured to comply with current (2026) NIL guidelines. If you're a high school athlete in a state with restrictions, we walk you through what you can and can't do. Your eligibility is not something we put at risk.
Can I upgrade from one package to another later?
Yes. Start at any tier, move up whenever you want — you only pay the difference. Everything you've built along the way carries forward.
Can I cancel the Development Retainer?
Yes. Cancel anytime — the Development Retainer is a month-to-month arrangement, not a contract.
